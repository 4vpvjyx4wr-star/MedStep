1. Which physician is most closely associated with the description, in
1916, of the seven-stage shoulder articulatory sequence that still bears
his name?
A. Charles Spencer
B. A. T. Still
C. George Laughlin
D. John Martin Littlejohn
Answer. A. Charles Spencer
Explanation. Spencer described the seven-stage shoulder sequence in
1916, which remains the standard articulated protocol. Still founded
osteopathy. Laughlin and Littlejohn were early academic figures, but did
not author the sequence.
Section 2 · Multiple Choice with
Explanations

===== PAGE 5 =====
2. Which feature most distinguishes articulatory technique from HVLA
when both begin from the same diagnostic setup?
A. Articulatory technique is indirect; HVLA is direct.
B. Articulatory technique substitutes repeated low-velocity arcs through
the barrier for a single high-velocity thrust.
C. Articulatory technique uses isometric patient contractions while
HVLA is passive.
D. Articulatory technique requires patient breath-holding, while HVLA
does not.
Answer. B. Articulatory technique substitutes repeated low-velocity
arcs through the barrier for a single high-velocity thrust.
Explanation. Both are direct techniques that engage the restrictive
barrier. The defining contrast is between velocity and repetition:
articulatory delivers repeated, low-velocity, moderate-amplitude
motion, while HVLA delivers a single, low-amplitude, high-velocity
impulse.
3. Which accessory force is the default in articulatory technique and is
a key contrast with the Still technique?
A. Compression along the long axis of the lever
B. Rotational torque at the segment
C. Long-axis traction
D. Isometric muscle contraction by the patient
Answer. C. Long-axis traction

===== PAGE 6 =====
Explanation. Articulatory technique defaults to long-axis traction and
generally avoids compression. The still technique relies on a small
facilitating compression vector through the corrective arc. This single
distinction allows the techniques to be told apart at the bedside.
4. Which palpatory finding is the principal indicator that an articular
somatic dysfunction is likely to respond to articulatory technique?
A. Tender point at a fascial trigger site
B. Boggy edema in the paravertebral tissues
C. A clear position of ease in three planes
D. Decreased accessory motion (joint play) with a firm articular barrier
Answer. D. Decreased accessory motion (joint play) with a firm
articular barrier
Explanation. Articulatory technique addresses the arthrodial
component of somatic dysfunction. A firm articular barrier with reduced
joint play is the ideal target. A rubbery or vague barrier often responds
better to counterstrain, myofascial release, or muscle energy.
5. Within an integrated treatment plan, which of the following is NOT a
recognized role for articulatory technique?
A. Replacement for accurate diagnosis when TART findings are unclear
B. Preparatory technique before HVLA at the same segment
C. Primary corrective technique when HVLA is declined or
contraindicated
D. Follow-up technique to integrate a newly mobile segment into a
regional arc

===== PAGE 7 =====
Answer. A. Replacement for accurate diagnosis when TART findings are
unclear
Explanation. Articulatory technique is applied only after a discrete
articular somatic dysfunction is identified. It can serve as a preparatory,
primary, or follow-up technique, but it is never a substitute for
diagnosis.
6. A patient cannot tolerate cervical HVLA. The clinician chooses the
articulatory technique at C4C5 instead. Which best describes the
appropriate articulatory setup?
A. Use a completely different setup designed for muscle energy
B. Use the position of ease alone, hold for 3 to 5 seconds, then release
C. Use the HVLA setup, then deliver repeated low-velocity arcs along
the facet plane instead of a thrust
D. Use seated thoracic landmarks because cervical articulatory
technique is contraindicated
Answer. C. Use the HVLA setup, then deliver repeated low-velocity
arcs along the facet plane instead of a thrust
Explanation. The HVLA-to-articulatory conversion rule applies: localize
and engage exactly as for HVLA, then spring repeatedly through the
barrier along the facet plane rather than delivering a single thrust.
7. Which option lists the seven stages of the Spencer sequence in the
conventional order?
A. Flexion, extension, abduction, internal rotation, circumduction with
compression, circumduction with traction, and inferior glide

===== PAGE 8 =====
B. Extension, flexion, circumduction with compression, circumduction
with traction, abduction, internal rotation behind the back, and inferior
glide with traction
C. Abduction, adduction, flexion, extension, internal rotation, external
rotation, circumduction
D. Extension, flexion, abduction, adduction, internal rotation, external
rotation, circumduction with thrust
Answer. B. Extension, flexion, circumduction with compression,
circumduction with traction, abduction, internal rotation behind the
back, and inferior glide with traction
Explanation. The classical Spencer order is extension, flexion,
circumduction with compression, circumduction with traction,
abduction, internal rotation behind the back, and inferior glide with
traction, performed at about 58 cycles per stage.
8. Which of the following is the most appropriate absolute
contraindication to articulatory technique at a given joint?
A. Suspected fracture at the joint or adjacent structures
B. Mild osteoporosis with intact bone integrity
C. Subacute mechanical low back pain
D. Mild apprehension about manipulation
Answer. A. Suspected fracture at the joint or adjacent structures
Explanation. Fracture or suspected fracture is an absolute
contraindication. Mild osteoporosis is a relative contraindication that
favors a low-amplitude technique. Subacute mechanical pain and
apprehension are common indications rather than contraindications.

9. Which statement best describes the supine sacroiliac circumduction
protocol?
A. Compression along the femoral axis is maintained throughout the
entire technique as the therapeutic force.
B. The hip is held in full extension while the SI joint is circumducted.
C. Three to five circumduction cycles are performed without any
traction at any point.
D. Brief compression along the femoral axis localizes the SI joint;
therapy is circumduction with long-axis traction, performed in both
directions.
Answer. D. Brief compression along the femoral axis localizes the SI
joint; therapy is circumduction with long-axis traction, performed in
both directions.
Explanation. In this protocol, the hip is flexed 45 degrees and abducted
30 to 45 degrees. Compression is only a brief localizing vector. The
therapeutic forces are circumduction and long-axis traction, repeated in
both rotational directions.
10. Which combination most accurately describes articulatory
technique?
A. Indirect, high velocity, low amplitude, single thrust
B. Direct, low velocity, moderate amplitude, repetitive without thrust
C. Indirect, static positioning held 90 seconds
D. Combined indirect to direct with a small facilitating compression
vector through the barrier

===== PAGE 10 =====
Answer. B. Direct, low velocity, moderate amplitude, repetitive without
thrust
Explanation. Articulatory technique is, by definition, direct (carries
motion toward and against the barrier), low-velocity, moderate-
amplitude, and repetitive, without thrust. Option D describes the Still
technique.
11. A 75-year-old woman with adhesive capsulitis of the right shoulder
has limited overhead range and apprehension toward HVLA. Which
articulatory protocol has the strongest peer-reviewed support for this
clinical setting?
A. Supine sacroiliac circumduction
B. Knee wobble Motyka variant)
C. Spencer sequence for the shoulder
D. Seated cervical articulatory technique at C5C6
Answer. C. Spencer sequence for the shoulder
Explanation. The Spencer sequence has the best technique-specific
evidence base for articulatory technique, supported by a small RCT by
Knebl in 2002 in elderly patients with shoulder dysfunction, along with
further small studies and pilot studies in adhesive capsulitis.
12. Which TART feature is least responsive in isolation as a marker for
articulatory technique efficacy, while restoration of joint play is the
most informative?
A. Tenderness alone in the absence of restriction or asymmetry
B. Restriction of motion across multiple planes

===== PAGE 11 =====
C. Tissue texture change at the segment
D. Asymmetry confirmed in active and passive testing
Answer. A. Tenderness alone in the absence of restriction or
asymmetry
Explanation. All four TART features should be reassessed, but
tenderness alone is the least specific for articular dysfunction.
Restoration of joint play and reduction of restriction are the most
informative articulatory endpoints.
13. Which feature is the single defining contrast between the
articulatory technique and the Still technique?
A. Articulatory technique is indirect; the Still technique is direct.
B. Articulatory technique uses isometric contraction; the Still technique
uses passive motion.
C. Articulatory technique uses a single corrective force; the Still
technique uses repetitive motion.
D. Articulatory technique uses long-axis traction; the Still technique
uses a small facilitating compression vector along the long axis of the
lever.
Answer. D. Articulatory technique uses long-axis traction; Still
technique uses a small facilitating compression vector along the long
axis of the lever.
Explanation. Both techniques are direct and apply force along the long
axis of the lever, and Still includes an indirect-to-direct arc. The defining
contrast is the accessory force: traction in articulatory, compression in
Still.

14. Which of the following best describes when long-axis traction
should be replaced by compression in articulatory technique?
A. When the patient is hospitalized
B. Long-axis traction is the default; compression is generally avoided in
articulatory technique. Substitution is not routine.
C. When treating a hypermobile sacroiliac joint
D. When the segment fails to release after two thrust attempts
Answer. B. Long-axis traction is the default; compression is generally
avoided in articulatory technique. Substitution is not routine.
Explanation. Avoidance of compression is a defining feature of
articulatory technique. If a clinician chooses to add compression, the
technique has become a different modality (such as Still). HVLA thrust
attempts are not part of articulatory practice.

of rib raising on sympathetic indicators (skin conductance, HRV.

16. Which statement about the supine knee wobble Motyka variant) is
correct?
A. The physician sits on a rolling stool, traps the foot between the
knees, and applies a single thrust.
B. The patient is prone, and the hand contact is over the popliteal fossa.
C. Compression along the long axis of the tibia is the primary
therapeutic force.
D. The patient is supine, the foot is trapped between the physician’s arm
and rib cage, and side-to-side femorotibial articulation is delivered
under continuous long-axis traction.
Answer. D. The patient is supine, the foot is trapped between the
physician’s arm and rib cage, and side-to-side femorotibial articulation
is delivered under continuous long-axis traction.
Explanation. The Motyka variant is supine with the foot trapped
between arm and rib cage, freeing both hands to deliver side-to-side
femorotibial articulation under continuous long-axis traction for 1 to 2
minutes. The traditional seated variant uses a rolling stool and the
physician’s knees.
17. Which is the most common procedural error that causes articulatory
technique to fail?
A. Poor localization with failure to stabilize the adjacent segment,
allowing motion to dissipate
B. Use of long-axis traction instead of compression

===== PAGE 14 =====
C. Choosing the same setup that would be used for HVLA
D. Beginning with a small amplitude before increasing amplitude as the
joint relaxes
Answer. A. Poor localization with failure to stabilize the adjacent
segment, allowing motion to dissipate
Explanation. Localization is the most common reason why articulatory
techniques fail. The other listed options describe correct practice
(traction default, conversion from HVLA setup, and graded amplitude).
18. Which integrated framework is most commonly cited to explain the
clinical effects of manual therapy techniques, including articulatory
techniques?
A. Cartesian dualistic model
B. Gate control theory in isolation
C. Bialosky's integrated model of biomechanical, neurophysiological,
circulatory, and contextual mechanisms
D. Endogenous opioid cascade model alone
Answer. C. Bialosky's integrated model of biomechanical,
neurophysiological, circulatory, and contextual mechanisms
Explanation. The Bialosky integrated model proposes that mechanical
input from manual therapy initiates peripheral and central
neurophysiological responses, modulated by circulatory and contextual
factors. It maps directly onto articulatory technique and is the most
widely cited contemporary framework.
Section 3 · Clinical Vignettes with
Explanations

===== PAGE 15 =====
1. A 68-year-old man recovering from open colectomy on post-op day
2 has reduced rib excursion, splinted breathing, and post-operative
ileus. The team requests an osteopathic technique that may improve
respiratory mechanics, mobilize the rib cage, and potentially reduce
sympathetic outflow without thrust. Which articulatory technique is
most appropriate, and how is it performed?
Answer. Rib raising. With the patient supine and the physician seated at
the patient's side, the finger pads contact the paravertebral tissues just
lateral to the thoracic transverse processes T1 through T12, as
indicated). The physician applies a gentle anterior and lateral lever,
using the table as a fulcrum, lifting the posterior ribs into external
rotation; release and repeat in a slow rhythmic cadence for about 1 to 2
minutes per region, coordinating with the patient’s respiratory cycle.
Bilateral or paired-segment variants are appropriate.
Explanation. Rib raising is an articulatory protocol specifically indicated
for hospitalized and postoperative patients with reduced rib excursion
and ileus. It is low-velocity, repetitive, and does not require thrust,
making it well-suited to a post-op abdomen. It is often combined with
thoracic inlet release and pedal pump in lymphatic protocols.
2. A 24-year-old endurance runner has unilateral right innominate
anterior somatic dysfunction with associated SI restriction. She declines
HVLA. Which two articulatory protocols, in sequence, are best matched
to her presentation, and what are the key technical points for each?
Answer. First, supine sacroiliac circumduction. Flex the right hip to
about 45 degrees, abduct 30 to 45 degrees, apply brief femoral-axis
compression to localize the SI joint, then deliver 3 to 5 circumduction
cycles. Slide the hand distally and apply a long-axis traction tug;
reverse direction. Second, the lateral-recumbent innominate articulatory
technique with the dysfunctional side up: coordinated springing at the
ASIS and ischial tuberosity at end-exhalation, mirroring the sideline
pelvis HVLA setup but without a thrust.

===== PAGE 16 =====
Explanation. The two protocols address adjacent components of the
dysfunction. Supine SI circumduction mobilizes the SI joint with traction
as the therapeutic force, while the lateral-recumbent innominate
technique addresses the pelvis as a whole through a convertible HVLA
setup. Together, they provide the integrated regional treatment that the
articulatory technique is well-suited to deliver.
3. A 55-year-old woman with adhesive capsulitis of the left shoulder
has progressive loss of overhead range, sleep disturbance, and
apprehension about manipulation. Which articulatory protocol is most
appropriate, what is the stage order, and what evidence supports use?
Answer. Spencer sequence for the shoulder. With the patient in left
lateral recumbent, with the affected (left) shoulder up (or in some
variations, supine), perform the following stages in order: extension,
flexion, circumduction with compression, circumduction with traction,
abduction, internal rotation behind the back, and inferior glide with
traction. Perform 5 to 8 cycles at each stage. Stabilize the scapula
throughout. Knebl’s 2002 RCT in elderly patients with shoulder
dysfunction (n=29 showed significantly improved range of motion in
the Spencer group, with improvement continuing post-treatment, while
the placebo group declined.
Explanation. Spencer is the paradigm articulatory protocol and has the
best technique-specific evidence base in articulatory technique. The
lateral-recumbent setup stabilizes the trunk and frees both hands for
stage-specific stabilization and motion delivery.
4. A 78-year-old hospitalized woman with moderate osteoporosis has
T6 ERSr somatic dysfunction limiting rib excursion. The hospitalist
requests an osteopathic technique that will address the thoracic
segmental dysfunction itself, not just the rib cage. HVLA is
contraindicated due to bone density and apprehension. How would you
perform the articulatory technique at this segment?

===== PAGE 17 =====
Answer. Supine, arms crossed (the same setup used for supine thoracic
HVLA. Use a thenar contact at the left transverse process of T6 (since
the segment is rotated right, the left side is restricted) to localize. Bring
the patient over the contact point and spring through the patient’s
crossed elbows at end-exhalation with low-velocity repeated arcs.
Substitute repeated springing for the HVLA thrust. Coordinate with
respiration and stop once a measurable improvement in segmental
motion is achieved.
Explanation. This is the HVLA-to-articulatory conversion in clinical
practice: identical localization and setup, but the corrective force is
applied as repeated low-velocity arcs through the barrier rather than as
a thrust. The technique is well-suited to elderly and frail patients in
whom HVLA is undesired or contraindicated.
5. A 62-year-old man with chronic mechanical low back pain has
bilateral L4L5 and L5S1 restriction, prefers lying on his side, and is
moderately apprehensive about HVLA. Which lumbar articulatory setup
is most appropriate, and how is it delivered?
Answer. Lateral-recumbent lumbar articulatory technique (the same
setup used for lateral-recumbent lumbar HVLA. Place the patient on
the side with the dysfunctional side up. Use coordinated anterior-pelvic
and posterior-shoulder springing at end-exhalation, delivering repeated
low-velocity arcs through the barrier rather than a single thrust. Perform
on both sides if indicated and reassess between rounds.
Explanation. The lateral-recumbent setup gives leverage for combined
rotational and sidebending forces while remaining comfortable for
apprehensive patients. The technique is a direct conversion of the
lumbar HVLA setup using the conversion rule.
6. A 34-year-old woman with chronic neck pain has C5C6 restriction
with normal vertebral artery screening. She has had previous HVLA and

===== PAGE 18 =====
prefers a non-thrust approach today. How would you select and deliver
an articulatory technique at this segment?
Answer. Use the supine or seated cervical setup that mirrors cervical
HVLA at C5C6. Localize with a translatory cradle hold (occiput
supported in the palm, fingers along the articular pillars) or seated chin-
hand support. Carry the segment to the barrier in the facet plane (a line
that approximates the angle of the mandible) and deliver repeated low-
velocity arcs along that plane rather than a thrust. Reassess between
rounds. Always confirm vertebral artery screening before any cervical
articulation.
Explanation. Cervical articulatory technique applies the conversion rule
to the HVLA setup. Vertebral artery and foraminal compression
screening must be completed before any cervical articulation, and
amplitude should remain low given the proximity of vital structures.
7. A 70-year-old man with knee osteoarthritis (moderate) and persistent
anterior knee stiffness has decreased patellofemoral and tibiofemoral
accessory motion. Which articulatory technique is appropriate, and
what is the role of long-axis traction in the chosen approach?
Answer. Knee wobble. Either the traditional seated variant (physician
on a rolling stool, the patient’s foot trapped between the physician’s
knees) or the supine Motyka variant (foot trapped between the
physician’s arm and rib cage). Deliver continuous long-axis traction at
the tibia while gently articulating the femorotibial joint from side to side
in small arcs for 1 to 2 minutes. The traction is therapeutic, not just
stabilizing: it unloads the joint surfaces and allows the side-to-side
articulation to mobilize the joint without compressing arthritic surfaces.
Explanation. Long-axis traction is the default accessory force in
articulatory technique. In an osteoarthritic knee, it serves a dual role of
unloading the joint and providing the therapeutic vector that allows

===== PAGE 19 =====
mobilization without compression. Patellofemoral circumduction may be
added in the same session.
1. List, in order, the six general treatment steps of articulatory
technique.
Model answer. 1 Diagnose the articular somatic dysfunction. Identify
the restrictive barrier and position of ease in all relevant planes and
confirm decreased accessory motion. 2 Position the patient and
yourself for joint isolation. Stabilize proximal or distal segments so
motion is delivered to the target joint, and use whole-body mechanics.
3 Engage the joint within its physiologic range. Begin with a small,
gentle arc. In irritable tissues, start in ease before progressing to the
barrier; in less irritable tissues, engage the barrier directly. 4 Apply
repetitive, rhythmic motion at low velocity and moderate amplitude.
Apply long-axis traction as the default accessory force; compression is
generally avoided. Choose linear springing (convertible from any HVLA
or muscle energy setup) or circumduction with traction through a
multidirectional arc. 5 Reassess continuously during treatment. Modify
amplitude, frequency, or direction in response to tissue feedback. Stop
or switch modality if pain exceeds mild discomfort. 6 Return to neutral
and reassess TART. Confirm restoration of joint play and document.
2. State the HVLA-to-articulatory conversion rule and explain in two or
three sentences why it is the most useful single principle in the
integrated osteopathic toolkit.
Model answer. The rule: any HVLA or muscle energy setup can be
converted to an articulatory technique by substituting repeated low-
velocity arcs through the barrier for the single corrective force.
Diagnose, position, and engage the barrier; then spring rather than
thrust. The rule is uniquely useful because it lets a single set of regional

3. Compare and contrast the articulatory technique and the Still
technique in two to three sentences, focusing on the defining contrast.
Model answer. Both techniques are direct, low-to-moderate-velocity,
and apply force along the long axis of the lever, and still include an
indirect-to-direct arc. The defining contrast is the accessory force:
articulatory technique defaults to long-axis traction. Generally, it avoids
compression, while the Still technique uses a small facilitating
compression vector along the long axis of the lever throughout the
corrective arc. Articulatory technique is repetitive (multiple cycles
through the barrier); the Still technique is a single composite arc carried
in all three planes.

5. Summarize the current evidence base for articulatory technique in
three to five sentences, including the best-studied protocol, the
autonomic study domain, and the broader manual therapy literature.
Model answer. The Spencer sequence has the strongest technique-
specific data, anchored by Knebl’s 2002 RCT (n=29 in elderly patients
with shoulder dysfunction, which showed significantly improved range
of motion in the Spencer group that continued to increase post-
treatment while the placebo group declined; subsequent comparative
studies and pilots continue to support the Spencer technique for
adhesive capsulitis. Rib raising has been studied for its short-term
effects on sympathetic indicators (skin conductance, HRV in healthy
volunteers and selected inpatient populations, with mixed but
suggestive results supporting continued use in postoperative ileus and
reduced rib excursion. Whole-system OMT, of which articulatory
technique is a frequent component, has moderate-quality evidence for
reducing pain and improving function in acute neck pain, chronic non-
specific low back pain, low back pain in pregnancy, and chronic non-
oncologic pain; the AOA low-back-pain clinical practice guideline cites
moderate-quality evidence with effect sizes comparable to NSAIDs.
Outside the osteopathic literature, joint mobilization, which shares

===== PAGE 22 =====
biomechanical and neurophysiological principles with articulatory OMT,
has supporting data for low back pain, neck pain, and shoulder
dysfunction, particularly when combined with exercise Hurwitz and
others). Honest framing: articulatory technique is the most
generalizable osteopathic technique; the Spencer sequence provides
the best technique-specific data; and the broader OMT and manual
therapy literature provides moderate-quality support for joint
mobilization more generally.
