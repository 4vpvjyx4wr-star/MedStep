2. Which cranial nerve carries parasympathetic fibers to the lacrimal
and nasal glands?
A. CN III
B. CN V
C. CN VII
D. CN IX
Answer. C. CN VII
Explanation. The greater petrosal branch of the facial nerve CN VII
carries preganglionic parasympathetic fibers that synapse in the
pterygopalatine (sphenopalatine) ganglion before innervating lacrimal,
nasal, and palatine glands. The head and neck receive parasympathetic
supply from four cranial nerves: CN III (ciliary ganglion → eye), CN VII
(pterygopalatine ganglion → lacrimal/nasal; submandibular ganglion →
salivary glands), CN IX (otic ganglion → parotid), and CN X (vagus →
pharynx/larynx).

A. CN V
B. CN VII
C. CN IX
D. CN X
Answer. A. CN V

the face and most of the upper airway through its three divisions V1
ophthalmic, V2 maxillary, V3 mandibular).
4. A child with recurrent acute otitis media is brought in for OMT
between episodes. Which technique most directly addresses
pharyngotympanic tube function?
A. Suboccipital release

17. Which is an absolute contraindication to OMT in a patient with EENT
complaints?
A. Mild allergic rhinitis on intranasal steroids
B. Suspected basilar skull fracture with clear rhinorrhea
C. Stable TMJ clicking
D. Treated chronic sinusitis
Answer. B. Suspected basilar skull fracture with clear rhinorrhea
Explanation. Clear rhinorrhea after head trauma raises concern for CSF
leak and basilar skull fracture. OMT is absolutely contraindicated until
the structural injury is excluded and stabilized.

18. Which statement about evidence for OMT in EENT conditions is
most accurate?
A. OMT replaces antibiotics in confirmed bacterial sinusitis
B. Adjunctive OMT may reduce recurrence and effusion in pediatric
otitis media in small trials
C. OMT cures allergic rhinitis
D. OMT eliminates the need for tympanostomy tubes in all patients
Answer. B. Adjunctive OMT may reduce recurrence and effusion in
pediatric otitis media in small trials
Explanation. Small RCTs Mills 2003 and a JAOA effusion trial Steele
2014 suggest adjunctive OMT may reduce recurrence and middle ear
effusion in children with recurrent acute otitis media. OMT is adjunctive
and does not replace pharmacotherapy or surgery when indicated.

1. A 4-year-old presents with his fourth episode of acute otitis media in
twelve months. Examination today shows a dull, retracted left tympanic
membrane without bulging or fever. He is up to date on guideline-based
pharmacotherapy. Which adjunctive OMT technique most directly
addresses pharyngotympanic tube function?
A. HVLA to the cervical spine
B. Galbreath (mandibular pump)
C. Pedal pump only
D. Direct pressure over the mastoid
Answer. B. Galbreath (mandibular pump)
Explanation. Galbreath gently mobilizes the mandible and surrounding
tissue to support pharyngotympanic tube ventilation. HVLA is
contraindicated in children, and direct mastoid pressure is
inappropriate. The pedal pump is peripheral; central tube mechanics are
the priority here.
2. A 32-year-old with seasonal allergic rhinitis reports thick mucus,
blocked nasal passages, and sinus pressure. Examination shows TART
changes at T2T4 and in the OA region. Which sequence opens the
central pathway first?
A. Sphenopalatine contact, then HVLA T4
B. MFR thoracic inlet, then suboccipital release
C. Direct sinus percussion, then pedal pump
D. Counterstrain to the masseter, then the thoracic inlet

===== PAGE 16 =====
Answer. B. MFR thoracic inlet, then suboccipital release
Explanation. Treat centrally before peripherally. Open the inlet, then
balance vagal tone at the suboccipital region before progressing to
facial and sphenopalatine work.
3. A 6-month-old with otitis media with effusion is brought for
adjunctive osteopathic care. The anterior fontanelle is still open. Which

A. HVLA to C1

Answer. C. Indirect, low-force suboccipital and gentle cervical
lymphatic drainage
Explanation. Open fontanelles preclude HVLA and forceful cranial work.
Indirect, low-force, and lymphatic techniques are preferred. Direct
mastoid percussion has no role here.

effective.

Explanation. The mandible deviates toward the side of the hypertonic

16. A 29-year-old man presents with acute maxillary sinusitis. On

Answer: A. The upper edge of the 2nd rib, a few centimeters lateral to

Explanation: The anterior Chapman point for the sinuses lies on the
upper edge of the 2nd rib near the parasternal region (a few
centimeters lateral to the sternum). The C2 transverse process is the
posterior sinus point, and the mandibular angle, scapular spine, and
xiphoid are not Chapman locations.
17. A 34-year-old woman is treated for acute sinusitis. The physician
performs doming of the abdominal diaphragm. This technique produces
its principal therapeutic benefit through which of the following
mechanisms?

===== PAGE 24 =====
A. Raising sympathetic tone to constrict nasal blood vessels
B. Interrupting nociceptive afferents from the trigeminal nerve
C. Blocking neurogenic release of inflammatory mediators
D. Directly lowering parasympathetic outflow to the nasal mucosa
E. Enhancing venous and lymphatic return through increased

Answer: E. Enhancing venous and lymphatic return through increased

Explanation: Doming restores diaphragmatic excursion and the

change autonomic tone, block neurogenic inflammation, or interrupt

18. A 59-year-old woman with acute viral pharyngitis is treated with rib

B. Sympathetic tone
C. Lymphatic drainage
D. Neurogenic inflammation
E. Mucociliary beat frequency
Answer: B. Sympathetic tone

Explanation: Rib raising over T1 to T4 stimulates and then normalizes
the paravertebral sympathetic chain, so its most direct effect is
modulation of sympathetic tone. Other techniques address
parasympathetic tone OA to C2, lymphatic drainage, and neurogenic
inflammation, with mucociliary rate as a downstream rather than direct
target.
19. A 51-year-old man reports right-sided facial pressure over the
cheek during a bout of viral rhinitis. The physician applies gentle digital
pressure over the right infraorbital foramen. This maneuver most likely
benefits the patient by which of the following mechanisms?
A. Reducing sympathetic vasoconstriction of the sinus mucosa
B. Increasing motion of the maxillary bone
C. Directly reducing parasympathetic tone to the sinus mucosa
D. Thickening nasal secretions to reduce postnasal drip
E. Reducing neurogenic inflammation by inhibiting the V2 division of the
trigeminal nerve
Answer: E. Reducing neurogenic inflammation by inhibiting the V2
division of the trigeminal nerve
Explanation: The infraorbital foramen transmits the maxillary V2
division of the trigeminal nerve; inhibitory pressure there reduces the
neurogenic inflammatory signaling responsible for facial pain and
congestion. It is not intended to move bone, thicken mucus, or directly
alter autonomic tone.

2. Autonomic balance in EENT disease. Explain how autonomic
imbalance contributes to symptom expression in upper airway

Sample answer. Sympathetic supply to head and neck structures arises
from T1 to T4 and ascends through the superior cervical ganglion.

4. Sinusitis and the central pathway. A patient presents with chronic
rhinosinusitis. Outline an evidence-aligned osteopathic approach and

Sample answer. The approach is adjunctive to medical management,

thoracic inlet, then suboccipital release to support vagal tone and
cervical drainage, then paraspinal inhibition T1 to T4 to modulate

forceful pressure when orbital or intracranial extension is suspected.
5. Galbreath technique. Describe the rationale, indication, and basic
execution of the Galbreath (mandibular pump) technique.
Sample answer. Galbreath, also known as the mandibular pump, applies
a gentle traction and pumping motion at the mandible to mobilize the
temporomandibular and parapharyngeal soft tissue and to support
pharyngotympanic tube ventilation. Indications include recurrent acute
otitis media and otitis media with effusion in pediatric patients between
acute episodes. With the patient supine, the operator cradles the
mandible, applies gentle inferior and lateral traction toward the affected
side, and rhythmically releases the contact. Pratt-Harrington JAOA
2000 provides a classic description of the technique.

9. Cranial considerations. Explain how cranial mechanics influence
EENT function and which structures merit attention.
Sample answer. The temporal bones house the auditory and vestibular
apparatus and articulate with the sphenoid and occiput. Sphenobasilar
synchondrosis strain patterns alter the position of the temporal bones,
the angle of the pharyngotympanic tube, and venous sinus drainage.
The occipitomastoid suture lies near the jugular foramen, where cranial
nerves IX, X, and XI exit. Gentle balancing of the sphenobasilar
synchondrosis, temporal bones, and occipitomastoid region can
support drainage and autonomic balance. Indirect, low-force technique
is preferred, and forceful cranial work is avoided in children with open
fontanelles and in any patient with suspected fracture or intracranial
pathology.
10. Red flags in EENT practice. List red flags that should prompt
immediate referral and preclude OMT in the EENT patient.
Sample answer. Sudden vision loss, painful red eye, or new diplopia.
Sudden sensorineural hearing loss or vertigo with neurologic deficit.
Severe headache with focal neurologic findings, papilledema, or
meningismus. Postauricular swelling with protruding pinna, suggesting
mastoiditis. Suspected basilar skull fracture or CSF rhinorrhea. Orbital
cellulitis or abscess. Persistent unilateral epistaxis, fixed cervical

Signs of meningitis or peritonsillar abscess. Refer urgently and defer
